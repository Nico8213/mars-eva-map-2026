# Research card: What and where Jezero is
- Finding: Jezero is an impact crater about 45-50 km across (a NASA release says 45 km; the Mars 2020 photogeologic mapping paper says about 50 km). It is interpreted as a Noachian-aged lake basin inside the western edge of the Isidis impact structure. A river delta fans out at the western edge of the crater, fed by a headwater region just outside it. NASA/JPL statements reported by news outlets say a lake about 500 m (1,600 ft) deep filled the crater between 3.5 and 3.9 billion years ago, fed by a river that flowed into and out of the crater.
- Source URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7116714 ; https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-landing-site-for-mars-2020-rover/ ; https://phys.org/news/2018-11-nasa-ancient-martian-river-delta.html ; https://astronomynow.com/2018/11/19/nasa-selects-jezero-crater-for-mars-2020-landing-site/
- Organization / author: Mars 2020 Science Team (photogeologic map paper, authors not retrieved); NASA (release); AFP and Astronomy Now (reports of NASA/JPL statements)
- Version / date: NASA release 2018-11-19; mapping paper in Space Science Reviews, date not checked
- Accessed on: 2026-11-14
- License: Not found on the pages I checked
- Confidence: high for the setting; medium for the lake depth and age (news reports of NASA statements, not the primary paper); crater size differs by source

# Research card: Why NASA chose Jezero
- Finding: NASA announced Jezero as the Mars 2020 landing site on 19 Nov 2018, after a five-year process in which more than 60 candidate sites were examined. Reasons given: a lake-delta system, abundant carbonate rocks, and a delta that is good at preserving evidence of life. JPL said the site offers at least five kinds of rock, including clays and carbonates. The geologic diversity also made landing hard, so NASA added Terrain Relative Navigation (TRN) for the landing. Perseverance landed in Jezero on 18 Feb 2021.
- Source URL: https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-landing-site-for-mars-2020-rover/ ; https://www.marsdaily.com/reports/NASA_picks_ancient_Martian_river_delta_for_2020_rover_touchdown_999.html ; https://astronomynow.com/2018/11/19/nasa-selects-jezero-crater-for-mars-2020-landing-site/

# Research card: Mapped landforms around the landing site
- Finding: The Mars 2020 Science Team made a photogeologic map of the landing site before launch. Its delta units include several layered types (blocky, thinly layered, thickly layered, truncated curvilinear layered and layered rough). The western fan (western delta) corresponds to the unit called "Jezero fan 2" in an earlier scheme, and the northern fan to "Jezero fan 1". The table also lists channel sands (inverted channel bodies) and scroll bars (point bar strata).

# Research card: Crater floor rocks are igneous (Seitah and Maaz)
- Finding: Before landing, scientists debated whether the Jezero floor was sediment or lava. Perseverance found the floor consists of igneous rocks altered by water. The lowest exposed unit, Seitah, is a coarsely crystalline olivine-rich rock that accumulated at the base of a magma body (an igneous cumulate). The overlying unit, Maaz, is interpreted as lava flows or the chemical complement to Seitah in a layered igneous body. The floor rocks lie below the delta sediments, so their crystallization ages will give an upper limit for when the delta formed.
- Source URL: https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/aqueously-altered-igneous-rocks-sampled-on-the-floor-of-jezero-cr/ ; https://searchworks.jioinstitute.edu.in/articles/cmedm__36007009 ; https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/962511
- Organization / author: Farley, K. A., Stack, K. M., Shuster, D. L., et al., "Aqueously altered igneous rocks sampled on the floor of Jezero crater, Mars", Science 377(6614), eabo2196; UC Berkeley news release
- Version / date: Science, 2022-09-30, DOI 10.1126/science.abo2196
- Accessed on: 2026-11-14
- License: Not found on the pages I checked
- Confidence: high

# Research card: Water alteration of the crater floor
- Finding: A summary of the first floor campaign (sols 100-379) says the Maaz and Seitah formations are igneous, made of five and two geologic members respectively. They preserve evidence of several episodes of water alteration, shown by secondary minerals such as carbonate, Fe/Mg phyllosilicates, sulfates and perchlorate. Seitah reacted with carbonated water, while Maaz holds pockets of minerals that may have formed from salty brine. News coverage of the Science papers says Seitah rocks formed underground and were exposed after covering rock eroded over the past 2.5 to 3.5 billion years.
- Source URL: https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/7p9d6-tge71 ; https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/08/220825164040.htm ; https://www.newswise.com/articles/perseverance-rover-retrieves-key-rocky-clues-to-mars-geologic-and-water-history
- Organization / author: Sun, V. Z., Hand, K. P., Stack, K. M., Farley, K. A., et al., "Overview and Results From the Mars 2020 Perseverance Rover's First Science Campaign on the Jezero Crater Floor" (Caltech record); UC Berkeley and Newswise news releases
- Version / date: paper copyright 2023; news releases Aug 2022
- Accessed on: 2026-11-14
- License: The Caltech record shows a copyright and license section (text not read); news pages: not found
- Confidence: high for the mineral list; medium for the erosion timescale (news report)

# Research card: Delta architecture and lake history
- Finding: Perseverance images of cliff faces at the delta edge (the Kodiak butte) show layers that pass from horizontal to inclined and back to horizontal, the pattern of topset, foreset and bottomset beds in a delta. The authors conclude the delta advanced into a lake whose depth varied over time, with steady flow into the lake at first (consistent with a warm, humid climate about 3.7 billion years ago) followed by flood deposits with large boulders. A later study of the western fan front confirms its deltaic nature and says the boulder deposits show intense flood episodes after the foreset and topset beds formed; their timing relative to the lake is still undetermined.
- Source URL: https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/930412 ; https://thedebrief.org/jezero-crater-river-delta/ ; https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/165734
- Organization / author: Mangold, N., Gupta, S., et al., "Perseverance rover reveals an ancient delta-lake system and flood deposits at Jezero crater, Mars", Science 374(6568), 2021; Mangold, N., Caravaca, G., et al., JGR Planets 129, e2023JE008204 (2024)
- Version / date: Science 2021-10-07; JGR Planets 2024-03-13
- Accessed on: 2026-11-14
- License: The MIT repository copy of the 2021 paper is listed as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; other pages: not found
- Confidence: high

# Research card: Delta front campaign and rock units
- Finding: Perseverance reached the foot of the western fan in late April 2022 (about sol 422). Between April and December 2022 it examined the basal delta layers at two locations about 400 m apart, called Hawksbill Gap and Cape Nukshak. A conference abstract says the sedimentary rocks of the "Shenandoah formation" are compositionally and mineralogically diverse, which means conditions changed in space or time while the delta formed.
- Source URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230000534 ; https://goldschmidtabstracts.info/2023/18487.pdf
- Organization / author: Pilleri, P. et al. (SuperCam overview, LPSC 2023 abstract); Hurowitz, J. et al. (PIXL, Goldschmidt 2023 abstract)
- Version / date: abstracts from March and July 2023
- Accessed on: 2026-11-14
- License: Not found on the pages I checked
- Confidence: medium (conference abstracts, not peer-reviewed papers)

# Research card: Minerals seen from orbit in the delta sediments
- Finding: A NASA caption says that orbital spectral data (from MRO's CRISM, combined with the Context Camera) show the Jezero delta sediments contain clays and carbonates, minerals that formed through water alteration. A published CRISM study of the Jezero region is described in vn-crism.md ("Olivine-Carbonate Mineralogy of the Jezero Crater Region").
- Source URL: https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-landing-site-for-mars-2020-rover/ ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.11414
